# Project North Star — Power, Enjoyment, and Capability

**Repository version introduced:** 2.9  
**Updated:** July 25, 2026  
**Status:** Required purpose and priority policy for Brent's Destiny 2 build-and-armory project  
**Companion files:** `Brents-Destiny-2-AI-Bootstrap.md`, `docs/Brent-Decision-Doctrine.md`, `docs/Brent-Personal-Evidence-Ledger.md`

## 1. Mission

The project exists to help Brent **maximize enjoyment and power in Destiny 2 by creating the strongest, most fun, and most capable builds possible for the content he cares about**.

The vault is not the product. It is infrastructure. Its job is to behave like a **high-performance armory** that makes excellent loadouts easier to build, understand, improve, and deploy.

A clean vault that produces weaker or less enjoyable builds is a failure. A large vault full of theoretical coverage that obscures the best tools is also a failure.

## 2. Success metrics

Use these in order:

1. **Build power and enjoyment:** Does the project create stronger, more enjoyable, more reliable, and more capable loadouts that help Brent beat desired content?
2. **Inventory clarity and reduced decision fatigue:** Can Brent understand what each important weapon is for, choose confidently, and see where upgrades matter?
3. **Vault space:** Is the armory operationally sustainable? Space is a distant tertiary metric, not the mission.

Do not report freed slots as the main outcome unless the task is specifically an emergency capacity problem. Report the build capability gained, clarified, protected, or unlocked.

## 3. Primary decision lens

For every meaningful item, build, or chase decision ask:

> **What new or stronger loadouts does keeping or deleting this enable?**

A keep should make an affirmative contribution:

- Unlock or complete a powerful build engine.
- Materially improve damage, survival, control, support, Champion handling, ammo economy, or rotation quality.
- Improve enjoyment, feel, reliability, or ease enough that Brent is more likely to use the build successfully.
- Provide a genuine specialist or encounter solution not covered better elsewhere.
- Compress several real roles into one excellent Tier 5 or selectable-perk item.
- Protect a costly capability that would matter if lost.

A deletion should make the armory better by:

- Removing a clearly outclassed or redundant option.
- Reducing false equivalence between a superior roll and weaker copies.
- Lowering decision fatigue and making the best tools easier to identify.
- Creating operational room and attention for a superior chase, craft, focus, catalyst, or enhancement.
- Eliminating museum pieces whose only defense is an ungrounded “maybe someday.”

## 4. Build engines before isolated weapons

Start with the build and content objective, then evaluate the weapon.

For each engine, identify:

- Class/subclass and Exotic armor.
- Content and difficulty.
- Core loop and failure/recovery behavior.
- Required weapon functions.
- Best owned options.
- Missing or weak components.
- The highest-impact obtainable upgrade.

Current Warlock engines receive special proactive attention because Brent explicitly prioritized them in this reframe. Hunter and other-class value still matter, especially when a weapon is portable or supports a real active build.

A famous weapon is not automatically important. A less famous weapon may be crucial if it provides the exact function a strong engine needs.

## 5. Power and fun must meet in actual use

Mechanical ceiling alone is not enough if Brent will not enjoy or reliably execute the build. Fun alone is not enough if the build cannot handle the intended content.

Evaluate:

- **Power:** How effectively does it kill, control, survive, support, or complete the encounter?
- **Enjoyment:** Does the loop feel satisfying, responsive, and worth repeating?
- **Reliability:** Does it work under realistic enemy density, difficulty, positioning, and failure conditions?
- **Capability:** What content or strategy becomes possible or easier?
- **Friction:** How much setup, downtime, execution burden, or resource cost is required?
- **Usage probability:** Is Brent likely to choose it over his alternatives?

These dimensions should be explained, not collapsed into an opaque score.

## 6. Protection is a threshold, not the goal

Keep the established safeguards for:

- Exact frames and firing behavior.
- Elements and origin-trait functions.
- Tier 5 legal configurations and complete stat packages.
- Raid, dungeon, Trials, Adept, event, retired, crafted, enhanced, and difficult-to-reacquire items.
- Favorites, use history, metadata, and explicit Brent statements.

These protections prevent careless deletion. They do not require permanent retention when an item is clearly outclassed, does not support a credible loadout, and would not realistically be chosen.

The correct posture is:

> Protect meaningful capability and personal value; do not protect redundancy merely because protection rules exist.

## 7. Proactive armory improvement

The AI is not only a cleanup assistant. It is a build and acquisition strategist.

Every major analysis should identify:

1. The highest-impact missing or weak armory capability.
2. What to chase, craft, focus, enhance, catalyst, or test.
3. Which build engine and content that target improves.
4. What current item it replaces, demotes, or complements.
5. Source difficulty, urgency, and expected payoff.

Prefer a short prioritized improvement plan over an unranked shopping list. A chase is valuable only when it has a clear build consequence.

## 8. Decision balance

Be more willing to delete when:

- A superior option exists for the same roles and engines Brent uses.
- The candidate's remaining advantage is theoretical or too small to affect an actual loadout.
- The item creates clutter or false choice without adding capability or enjoyment.
- A better obtainable target should become the clear focus.

Slow down when:

- A distinct frame, origin trait, element, or legal Tier combination changes the build.
- The item unlocks a niche but important encounter solution.
- Reacquisition is costly and the replacement case is not complete.
- Brent has strong personal evidence that the weapon improves enjoyment or execution.

Do not confuse caution with quality. The goal is the strongest justified decision.

## 9. Required outputs

A strong project output should answer:

- What are Brent's strongest current loadouts for the content being considered?
- Which armory pieces are essential, replaceable, or missing?
- Which owned rolls are clearly outclassed or redundant?
- What should Brent chase or craft next, in priority order?
- What new capability does each recommendation create?
- What uncertainty remains, and what single check would resolve it?

## 10. Final North Star

**Builds first. Armory second. Space third.**

The project succeeds when Brent has awesome loadouts he enjoys, understands why they work, can beat the content he cares about, and knows exactly which weapons deserve attention next.
