## The project in one paragraph

Brent has a very full Destiny 2 vault, a lot of duplicate weapons, and not enough time to compare every perk, frame, stat package, source, build interaction, and selectable Tier 5 combination by hand. The goal of this project is not to let an AI blindly decide which guns are “bad.” It is to build an evidence-based inventory system that can identify weapons Brent already owns that are genuinely redundant—while protecting unique roles, hard-to-reacquire gear, favorite weapons, build-enabling rolls, and anything whose value changes with class, activity, or playstyle.

## Why this is harder than “keep the god rolls”

A Destiny weapon is not just its name and two famous perks. Two copies of the same gun can differ in barrel, magazine, Masterwork, recoil behavior, range, stability, handling, reload speed, origin trait, enhancement path, source, and selectable perk combinations. Two guns of the same broad type can behave very differently because one is a slug shotgun and the other is a pellet shotgun, or one is a Wave Frame grenade launcher and the other is a conventional breech loader.

Even “best in slot” needs a complete sentence. There may be a best Solar support auto rifle for a particular kind of PvE build, but that does not automatically make every other auto rifle obsolete. A different element, frame, range band, Champion function, PvP role, or subclass interaction may justify another vault slot.

The central question is therefore not:

> Which gun has the lowest score?

It is:

> Which weapon has no realistic job that is not already covered better by another weapon Brent owns?

That is a much stricter and safer standard.

## Brent's situation and priorities

Brent uses Destiny Item Manager (DIM), describes himself as a pack rat, and has limited time for inventory maintenance. He wants meaningful space back, but he does not want a black-box score or a reckless mass-deletion list. He is conservative about irreversible dismantling, while still being willing to accept controlled risk when the reasoning and replacement coverage are clear.

The activities that matter most include difficult PvE, Grandmasters/Conquests, raids, dungeons, solo survival, and strong general-purpose builds. He generally values safe range, reliable perk activation, survivability, control, Champion utility, ammo economy, responsiveness, and weapons that minimize needless downtime. He can enjoy hard-hitting archetypes when a good roll fixes their sluggish handling, stability, or reload problems.

Warlock has historically been his most-developed class, but he recently started playing Hunter again. That matters because class abilities and builds can change weapon value. His comments about how a particular weapon feels, why he likes it, or what he is currently playing are treated as real project data—not disposable conversation.

## Tier 5 weapons make duplicate cleanup much more interesting

Some current weapons have several selectable perks in the same columns. A Tier 5 weapon cannot be judged only by whichever perks happen to be selected in DIM at the moment. It must be treated as a small collection of legal rolls contained in one physical item.

One copy might provide:

- A reload-and-damage combination for ordinary PvE
- A utility-and-damage combination for harder content
- A range-and-consistency combination for PvP
- A subclass or ability-economy combination for a particular build

That can let one excellent Tier 5 weapon replace several ordinary copies. But Tier 5 is not automatic immunity. A lower-tier copy can still be better if it has a superior perk combination or a materially better range, stability, handling, charge-time, draw-time, recoil, blade, guard, or magazine package.

This is why the project enumerates the useful legal combinations instead of counting gold borders or relying on the currently selected perks.

## Hunter dodge changed the reload conversation

One of Brent's recent realizations was that Hunter reload tools can change which rolls he prefers. Marksman's Dodge can reload all equipped weapons, so a conventional reload-speed perk on a primary weapon may be less valuable in a Hunter build than a utility, damage, survivability, or ability perk.

That does **not** mean every reload-related perk is obsolete. Passive reload perks can work while the weapon is holstered during a damage rotation. Overflow and reconstruction-style effects can prepare a magazine larger than normal. Subsistence and rewind-style perks can keep a weapon firing repeatedly without spending the dodge. The dodge itself also costs a class-ability charge that might otherwise support invisibility, Radiant, a melee loop, a decoy, positioning, or an Exotic armor effect.

The project therefore compares at least three kinds of value:

- **Portable value:** How good is the weapon without depending on one specific class or build?
- **Build-adjusted value:** How good is it in the Hunter, Warlock, Titan, subclass, Exotic, and activity currently being considered?
- **Rotation value:** Does it support weapon swaps, passive reloads, preloaded magazines, and damage timing?

This makes the analysis more complicated, but it prevents a temporary build choice from causing a permanent bad deletion.

## A few examples that shaped the system

### No Hesitation

A strong **Physic + Incandescent** No Hesitation is an excellent Solar support and add-clear benchmark. It can heal teammates and combine that role with Solar area damage. But “best for that role” does not necessarily mean Brent should keep exactly one No Hesitation. Another copy may support a distinct offensive, grenade, or high-difficulty role, especially when selectable Tier perks are involved.

### The Call

The Call now provides one of the clearest examples of why the project records Brent's own experience. His older favorite roll used Subsistence, which refunds ammunition after kills. He later recrafted the weapon with **Reconstruction** and found it dramatically better for him. While the weapon is stowed, Reconstruction can build its normal magazine of roughly 15 to approximately **30 micro-missiles**. He can use another weapon, pull The Call out, and have an absurdly large magazine already waiting—without first earning kills with it.

The lesson is larger than one perk: Brent strongly values proactive, low-friction readiness and fewer interruptions. The project also records what it does **not** know. Brent did not confirm the current fourth-column perk in the authoritative update, so the repository must not turn an earlier assistant guess into fact.

### Elsie's Rifle

Elsie's Rifle showed that Brent can enjoy a hard-hitting archetype when the roll repairs the parts that would otherwise feel slow or inconsistent. Recoil Direction alone cannot explain feel; stability, accuracy effects, magazine, handling, cadence, and input method all interact.

### Scathelocke

A Tier 5 Scathelocke can cover every desirable main-perk combination while a lower-tier copy still owns a better practical stat package. That is why perk-column dominance alone is not always enough for a “definitely delete” recommendation.

### Nessa's Oblation

A Void slug shotgun does not automatically replace every useful Void pellet shotgun. Exact firing behavior is a first-class role, not a cosmetic detail.

### Eager Edge swords

Movement weapons have special utility that a normal damage comparison can miss. The project permanently protects every functional Eager Edge category until a deliberate consolidation proves that at least one appropriate movement option remains.

### Bad Juju

Bad Juju really can contribute to Super generation, but it should not be treated as the automatic best answer. A stronger add-clear weapon may generate more total Super through faster kills, damage, Orbs of Power, and build interactions. The correct question is which weapon completes the entire Super-economy system, including the opportunity cost of using an Exotic weapon.

### Recoil Direction

The project also records the classic damped-sine explanation of Destiny's Recoil Direction stat. Values ending in 5 are centered nodes under that model, while the possible side bias generally shrinks as the stat approaches 100. A higher number is usually tighter, but a barrel or mod can move a weapon away from a centered value. Stability is a separate factor, so recoil is normally a close-tie consideration rather than a reason to discard an essential perk combination.

## How the AI is taught to think

The project now separates weapon rules from a **decision doctrine**. The doctrine tells a future AI how much attention a decision deserves.

An exact duplicate should not require an hour of research. A favorite crafted weapon, a rare dungeon drop, a complex Tier 5 roll, or a rule that could affect hundreds of items deserves much more scrutiny. For consequential deletions, the AI should make the strongest honest case for keeping the weapon, imagine what would cause regret later, and state what new evidence would reverse the conclusion.

It also labels where information came from. Something Brent directly said is different from a DIM observation, a verified mechanic, creator evidence, or an AI inference. That sounds bureaucratic, but it prevents a surprisingly dangerous failure: an assistant says something once, it gets copied into a document, and later the repository treats it as though Brent said it.

A separate Personal Evidence Ledger records active preferences and corrections. A State Reconciliation Protocol requires the AI to update every affected file and recommendation when Brent changes a weapon or corrects an assumption. In other words, the repository is not only trying to remember facts; it is trying to remember **how it knows them**.

## How outside opinions are used

Brent wants the project to study respected Destiny content creators and community recommendations, but not to treat popularity as proof. A creator recommendation is dated and evaluated for:

- The exact weapon, roll, role, build, and activity
- Whether the recommendation was demonstrated in relevant difficulty
- Whether it is current for the sandbox
- Whether several independent sources agree
- Whether it actually fits Brent's playstyle and inventory

This matters because creator opinions age. A weapon described as the best in one season may be outclassed after later perks, balance changes, or new loot. Community “god roll” lists are useful evidence, not commands.

## The safety system

The AI never dismantles anything. It can create a DIM import that tags a small set of weapons as `junk` for Brent to review, but Brent performs the final in-game action.

Before recommending deletion, the system is expected to:

1. Use the newest DIM export.
2. Preserve Brent's tags, notes, favorites, locks, crafted items, kill counts, loadouts, and sentimental signals.
3. Compare exact weapon versions, frames, firing behavior, elements, origin traits, and useful legal perk combinations.
4. Calculate the complete configured package, not just the main perks.
5. Identify the exact retained replacement.
6. Explain which roles are covered and what tradeoff, if any, is lost.
7. Account for source difficulty and reacquisition time.
8. Downgrade uncertain cases to manual review instead of pretending certainty.

Zero kills, an unlocked state, or missing DIM metadata are neutral. Positive evidence can protect an item, but missing evidence does not condemn it.

## Where the project currently stands

The permanent July 18 baseline contains **1,035 weapons across 495 unique names**. The July 19 working snapshot contains **1,073 weapons across 500 unique names**, including **231 duplicate-name groups**, **573 copies beyond the first copy of each name**, and **105 Tier 5 weapons**.

The current proposed DIM cleanup import contains only **seven conservative review candidates**. Older eight-item and 38-item proposals were archived because they are not the current recommendation. Sixteen cross-version candidates remain on hold for manual revalidation.

That small active list is intentional. The project began conservatively so that the analysis process could be audited and corrected before scaling to a larger dominance and best-in-role pass.

## The long-term goal

The end goal is not the smallest possible vault. It is a vault where each retained weapon has a reason to exist: a distinct role, a superior configuration, a class/build interaction, a hard-to-replace source, or personal value.

A successful cleanup should let Brent look at a proposed deletion and understand both sides:

- Why this copy appears redundant
- Which retained item replaces it
- What the replacement covers
- What small advantage might be lost
- How confident the analysis is

That is slower than blindly following a wishlist, but much faster and safer than Brent manually comparing more than a thousand weapons one at a time. It also turns the vault into a deliberate loadout toolbox rather than an unexplained collection of scores.
